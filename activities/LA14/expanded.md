# LA14. Personal AI Use Reflection — expanded version

The goal of this activity is to help students become aware of their own GenAI habits: when GenAI supports their learning, when it does the thinking for them, and when its use may contribute to the accumulation of cognitive debt. The activity is divided into three parts. Before a possible lecture on “AI Use and Cognitive Debt”, students will be asked to reflect on their daily use of generative AI by completing an individual survey. Then, students divide into small groups of two or three to discuss their individual responses, starting from some instructor's input. Finally, the whole class takes part in a discussion about the risks of cognitive debt and proposes strategies to mitigate them, such as writing first drafts without GenAI, explaining GenAI-generated outputs in their own words, practising skills they may be losing, and applying critical thinking.

This activity could also be integrated into an existing assignment in which the use of AI is permitted, such as a programming or writing task. Rather than reflecting on their general habits, students focus on how they used AI in that specific assignment by considering questions such as:

- Which parts were produced by AI?
- Which parts do I fully understand?
- Would I be able to complete a similar task again without AI?

This approach makes the reflection more concrete and can be easily incorporated into technical courses without requiring a separate session.

## Activity instructions

### Part 1 – Individual survey

- Think about how you have used generative AI tools in your daily life over the past two months, and answer the following questions:
  - Which generative AI tools have you used?
  - How often have you used them?
  - What tasks have you used them for?
- Reflect on your use of GenAI and answer the following questions:
  - Are there any skills—such as writing, debugging, summarising, planning, or translating—that you practise less frequently now?
  - Are there any tasks that you delegate entirely to GenAI? If so, which ones?
  - Which tasks would you now find difficult to complete without GenAI?
  - Do you engage in a dialogue with GenAI tools, or do you simply ask them questions?
  - Do you critically evaluate the outputs generated by GenAI tools?
- For each task you listed before, decide whether it falls into the category of “Augmentation”—GenAI helped you think or learn, for example by explaining a concept that you then understood—or “Substitution”—GenAI did the thinking for you, for example by writing code that you submitted without understanding it.
- Based on your categorisation, do you think you may be accumulating cognitive debt? If so, in which areas?

### Part 2 – Small-group discussion

Suggested reflection prompts:

- In which areas does cognitive debt accumulate most quickly?
- Which uses of GenAI are relatively unproblematic, and which may conceal risks?
- Think about how calculators made mental arithmetic less necessary, and reflect on the parallels with GenAI.

### Part 3 – In-class discussion

Suggested reflection prompts:

- Where are the main risks of cognitive debt likely to arise? In which areas and for which types of tasks?
- Can you propose strategies to mitigate these risks?
