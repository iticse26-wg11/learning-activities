# Personal AI Use Reflection

**Related ILOs:** EPR08
**Type:** In-class, Pre-sessional work, Individual and Small groups, Digital · **Duration:** ~20 min done at home + ~15–20 min in-class group discussion · **Scale:** 2 or more small groups of 4–5 students

> Expanded version: *(link to be added — placeholder in source)*

## Description

The goal of this activity is for students to become aware of their own AI habits: where AI helps them learn, where it does the thinking for them, and where they might be building up cognitive debt.

**Pre-sessional.** Before talking about "AI Use and Cognitive Debt", students are asked to look at their own GenAI use over a recent period (e.g., the past week or month): which tools did they use, for which tasks, how often, and what did they stop doing themselves as a result? Once they have a picture of how they use GenAI in their daily life, they answer some reflection questions, such as: Which tasks would I find hard to do now without AI? When did AI save me effort, and when did it do the thinking for me? Are there skills (writing, debugging, summarizing, planning) I practice less nowadays? Next, students sort their AI uses into two categories: **augmentation** (AI helped me think or learn, e.g., explaining a concept I then understood) and **substitution** (AI did the thinking for me, e.g., writing code I submitted without understanding it). Based on this, each student picks one or two areas where they might be building up cognitive debt.

**In class.** After the lecture on "AI Use and Cognitive Debt", students are divided into small groups and are instructed to discuss: Where does cognitive debt build up fastest? Which uses feel fine, and which feel harmful? Is some debt okay, in the same way calculators made mental math optional? Once the group discussions finish, the whole class discusses: common patterns are collected, along with ideas to counter them (e.g., writing first drafts without AI, explaining AI output in your own words, practicing skills you are losing).

**Variant.** This activity can be integrated into an existing assignment where AI use is allowed (e.g., a programming or writing task). Students then reflect on their AI use for that specific assignment rather than on their general habits: Which parts did AI produce? Which parts do I fully understand? Could I do this again without AI? This makes the reflection more concrete and is easy to add to technical courses without a separate session.
