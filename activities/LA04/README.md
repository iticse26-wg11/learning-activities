# Anatomy of (Another) AI System

**Related ILOs:** [EPR01](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/EPR01.md), [EPR09](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/EPR09.md)

**Type:** In-class, small groups, offline · **Duration:** 60 minutes · **Scale:** One or more small groups

## Description

Inspired by Crawford and Joler’s 2018 “Anatomy of an AI System” [(Crawford and Joler, 2018)](http://www.anatomyof.ai), student groups are tasked with researching, tracing, and relating (e.g., diagramming) the material, labor, and data resources involved in the training and deployment of a chosen (or assigned) real-world AI system (e.g., ChatGPT, self-driving car). Students leverage publicly available sources and reports to investigate the resource implications of the AI system, with the goal of quantifying or estimating the demand on key resources (e.g., total electricity cost of model training) and human labor (e.g., human AI data labelers, content moderators). When finished, students briefly summarize key findings, seeding a full-class discussion on the aggregate effects of growing demand for AI systems, especially in computing and software development domains. Discussions may also focus on the (often outsourced and underpaid) human labor required to train and sustain generative AI systems (from miners to data labelers to content moderators), broader impacts of what human work is valued, and whether the resource demand of AI systems is justifiable.

## Keywords

Resource consumption, labor, data, environmental impact, AI infrastructure

## Prerequisites

- Discussion of types of resources (raw materials, electricity and water, human labor, data) involved in AI systems and the costs (i.e., impacts) of their extraction

## Resources

- Poster paper, markers

## Assessment

Formative assessment evaluating depth and specificity of group’s research, and group’s ability to connect findings to each other and earlier class discussions/readings
