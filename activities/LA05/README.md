# AI Model Pipeline

**Related ILOs:** [MM02](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/MM02.md), [EPR03](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/EPR03.md) · **Prerequisite ILOs:** [CS02](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/CS02.md)

**Type:** In-class, individual and small groups, unplugged · **Duration:** 45 minutes · **Scale:** Suitable for small to large classes, can be completed individually, in small groups, or using a combination of individual work, group discussion, and a class debrief.

## Description

In small groups (or individually), students examine a simplified, high-level GenAI pipeline covering steps such as data collection, pre-training, alignment methods such as fine-tuning and reinforcement learning from human feedback, user prompts, token prediction, output generation, and data management across the pipeline. Because pipelines vary across GenAI systems, educators may focus on particular stages or adapt the pipeline to a specific system. The activity focuses on the purpose and implications of these stages rather than on their technical implementation.

Using a worksheet visualizing and briefly describes (at a high level) the pipeline, students identify associated limitations or risks, such as biased data, dataset cutoff dates, bias in human feedback, disclosure of sensitive information, misleading outputs, unclear data-retention practices, and loss of control over where data is stored, accessed, or used. Educators should emphasize that some limitations and risks may relate to more than one stage and that the activity does not require a one-to-one mapping between risks and pipeline stages.

Students then explain the model-development process in sequence, describe how development decisions can affect later outputs, and examine how data is collected, processed, stored, accessed, retained, or used across different stages of the pipeline. They evaluate who is responsible for preventing or addressing risks or misuse, manipulation, and threats to data sovereignty. This distinction helps separate limitations associated with model development from risks specifically related to data management.

Educators provide a brief high-level (conceptual) introduction to the GenAI pipeline before the activity and may use short examples or guiding questions to support discussion and address common misconceptions, based on the students' findings within the groups.
