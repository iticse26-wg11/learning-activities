# AI Application Project

**Related ILOs** (at the end of the course, students should be able to …)
- [EPR11](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/EPR11.md): design a plan for the responsible and context-appropriate use of GenAI, including documenting GenAI-related outputs and decisions.
- [EPR13](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/EPR13.md): discuss societal perceptions of and expectations around GenAI use, taking into consideration power dynamics in the adoption of GenAI tools.
- [EPR14](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/EPR14.md): discuss frameworks for the fair and responsible use of GenAI in the student's particular discipline.
- [CS07](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/CS07.md): apply prompt refinement techniques and evaluate outputs iteratively in order to improve alignment between generated content and intended outcomes.

**Type:** In-class, individual or small group, digital · **Duration:** 3 hours (90 min per session) · **Scale:** Small and large cohorts

## Description

This activity consists of two phases. The first phase is called “Plan and Create”, while the second phase is called “Reflect and Discuss”. In the first, students use GenAI to plan and execute a task. In the second, students reflect individually on their plans and results, and then discuss in groups. The two phases can be done in the same session or in individual sessions.

This activity aims to enable students to produce an artifact, critically reflect on the creation of the artifact while considering fair and responsible use of GenAI systems.

The goal topic of the task is to create a presentation (e.g., slides) of a specific subtopic of their studies. For example, for computer science students, the topic could be the relational database model, or password security. For Philosophy students, it could be related to some of Plato’s teachings. The goal is to have a topic related to the degree being studied. The complexity and scope should be short enough to fit in a couple of hours.

Tools: Students should only use chatbot GenAI systems (e.g., ChatGPT) to plan and execute this task. Students should not use more advanced GenAI-based tools, such as slide generators or specialized agents, during this task.

**Phase 1 - Plan and Create.**

**Task 1: Individual Planning.** Task 1: Individual Planning task - Students are tasked to come up with a plan on how they intend to delegate the task to the GenAI system (e.g., creation of the presentation, list of possible sources). It is important to instruct students to include in their plan some kind of validation steps (e.g., cross-checking references).

This planning task aims to promote students' approach GenAI in a structured manner, which should include, for example, output validation steps/mechanisms as well as use documentation mechanisms/formats. Furthermore, planning ahead makes students consider what interactions should and should not be done with GenAI (e.g., automating the content generation has different risks from automating the generation of slides).

**Task 2: Individual Prompting.** Students select a GenAI system of choice to complete this task iteratively until they are fine with the outcome. Students also submit a log of prompts and a reflection of their intentions behind each prompt.

**Phase 2 - Reflect and Discuss.**

**Task 3: Individual Reflection.** After completing the prompting task, students are asked to reflect through questions such as:

- Do you think that you learned the subtopic through this process?
- How would you describe the quality of the outcome provided initially and after prompt iteration?
- How would you rate the level of contribution of the final outcome between you and the GenAI system (e.g., 40% human, 60% GenAI)?
- Did you stick with your original plan? What changes did you make?

**Task 4: Discussion.** In class, students work in groups of 4-5 to discuss their experience considering societal perception and expectations around GenAI in producing educational material such as the one. Next, they review the OECD AI Principles, select two of their choice, and discuss how they relate to their discipline.

Note: It is possible to perform phase 1 (“Plan and Create”) at home, but we recommend that phase 2 (“Reflect and Discuss”) is done in class or in a similar proctored environment, to ensure that the student does not merely delegate the reflection to the GenAI system.
