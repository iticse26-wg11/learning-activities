# AI Mindmap

**Related ILOs:** [MM05](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/MM05.md)

**Type:** In-class, individual and small groups · **Duration:** 50 minutes · **Scale:** Any size of classroom

## Description

The goal of this activity is to make students' understanding and potential misconceptions about AI visible at the start of the course and, at the end of the course, to show them how their understanding of AI-related concepts has changed. This activity will be conducted in two sessions: one at the first session of the course and one at the very end. At the start of the course, students individually create a mindmap capturing their current understanding of AI on paper or digitally. They will receive initial prompts to consider what AI is, how it works, what it can and cannot do, or where it is used. The mindmap externalizes prior knowledge, assumptions, and potentially misconceptions without judgment; there are no "wrong" answers at this stage. Students should save those mindmaps because they will need them again for the second session of the activity. After students finish with the mindmaps they will discuss them in class as a motivating introduction to the topic. This activity can help educators identify potential misunderstandings or misconceptions in students’ current conceptions of AI and use these insights to inform subsequent teaching and learning activities.

During the last session of the course, students either revise their original mindmap (using a different color to mark additions, corrections, and deletions) or create a new one and compare it side-by-side with the original. Students reflect individually on the main changes they have made. This activity requires that students have engaged with AI technology throughout the course. Instructors will moderate a short reflection discussion on the comparison: What did they believe that turned out to be a myth? What capabilities did they over- or underestimate? What surprised them most?

When interpreting differences between the first and second mindmaps, instructors should not only consider conceptual understanding but also capture increased specificity and vocabulary. They should also be cautious when interpreting conceptual changes, because differences may also reflect memorized concepts or rapidly changing AI developments.

## Keywords

Prior knowledge, misconceptions, conceptual change, self-reflection, AI understanding

## Prerequisites

- Session 1: none
- Session 2: requires a first session (first mindmap) and access to the original one

## Resources

- Paper for creating mindmaps or whiteboard/mindmap tools
- Example prompts (e.g., "What is AI?" "How do you think AI works?" "What can AI do well?" “What limitations, risks, or concerns do you associate with AI?”)
