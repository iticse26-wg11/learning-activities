# AI Mindmap

**Related ILOs:** [MM05](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/MM05.md)
**Type:** In-class, Individual and Small groups · **Duration:** ~25 minutes (first session) + ~25 minutes (second session) · **Scale:** any size of classroom

## Description

The goal of this activity is to make students' beliefs about GenAI visible at the start of the course and, at the end of the course, to show them how their understanding has changed, especially which misconceptions they have left behind. This activity runs in two sessions: one at the first session of the course and one at the very end.

**Session 1 (start of course).** Students individually create a mindmap capturing their current understanding of GenAI on paper or digitally: what it is, how it works, what it can and cannot do, and where it is used. The mindmap externalizes prior knowledge, assumptions, and misconceptions without judgment; there are no "wrong" answers at this stage. Students should save those mindmaps because they will need them again for the second session of the activity. After students finish with the mindmaps they discuss them in class, and this leads into the lecture on "How GenAI works".

**Session 2 (end of course).** Students either revise their original mindmap (using a different color to mark additions, corrections, and deletions) or create a fresh one and compare it side-by-side with the original. A short reflection discussion on the comparison: What did they believe that turned out to be a myth? What capabilities did they over- or underestimate? What surprised them most?

## Keywords

Prior knowledge, Misconceptions, Conceptual change, Self-reflection, GenAI understanding

## Prerequisites

- Session 1: none
- Session 2: requires Session 1 and access to the original mindmaps

## Resources

- Paper for creating mindmaps, or an application like Miro
- Possibly some starter branches if students are stuck (e.g. "What GenAI is", "What it can do", "What it can't do", "Risks", "Examples of tools")

No
