# LA02. AI History Timeline — expanded version

In this activity, student groups must arrange a set of 10 ‘milestones’ in the history of artificial intelligence along a chronological timeline, without the aid of research or (much) prior knowledge. These provided milestones are concentrated in the 20th century or earlier and often have (purposefully) vague descriptions, emphasizing the long tail of AI history and connections between more recent developments in the field of AI and recurring themes of automation and conceptions of “intelligence”. After groups complete their timeline, an opportunity should be given for students to compare their work with other groups' decisions, before the instructor leads a combined class discussion during which the ‘true’ timeline (i.e. the years listed for each milestone below) is revealed. The timeline activity may end with a broader discussion of students' takeaways and surprises, as well as a more formal (and chronological) introduction to key stages in the development of ‘AI’.

## Activity instructions

Arrange the following ‘milestones’ in artificial intelligence on a timeline from 1900 to the 2020s (and beyond) according to when your group thinks the first example appeared or took place.

> **N.B.** For instructor convenience, the milestones below are presented in chronological order with the corresponding ‘solutions’. For the activity, students should be given *only* the titles of the milestones, in a randomized order.

### AI milestones

- **Chess-playing robot**
  - 1770: Wolfgang von Kempelen mystifies European royalty with the “Mechanical Turk”, a machine that moves chess pieces on a board to play against a human opponent. In reality, the robot was controlled by a chess master hidden in a secret compartment below the table.
- **Widespread coordinated worker protests against industrial automation**
  - 1810s: Textile workers in England protest against factory automation through weaving machines that undermined working conditions and output quality. Named “Luddites” after their fictional leader Ned Ludd, many rioters faced violent repression by industrialists and the state.
- **Model of the brain through neural ‘networks’**
  - 1943: McCulloch and Pitts publish “A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity”, describing the brain as “a net of neurons” that can be represented by computer logic gates.
- **High school algebra word problems solver**
  - 1964: Daniel Bobrow's PhD thesis project STUDENT uses rule-based logic to process and solve high school-level algebra word problems in ‘natural language’.
- **Computer ‘chatbot’**
  - 1966: Joseph Weizenbaum releases the ‘chatbot’ system ELIZA, which simulates a psychoanalyst by reflecting user input back in the form of a question.
- **Computer that can beat a chess grandmaster**
  - 1997: IBM's Deep Blue supercomputer beats grandmaster Garry Kasparov in a chess game by searching through possible future states of the board to determine the best move.
- **‘Self-driving’ car that carries passengers without a driver within specific city road systems**
  - 2017: Self-driving car company Waymo begins a pilot of its fully autonomous ‘robotaxi’ in Arizona, although semi-autonomous vehicle pilots have been tested over the past decades.
- **Multipurpose large language model (LLM)**
  - 2020s: Beginning with the popularization of Vaswani et al.'s 2017 ‘transformer’ neural network architecture, “large language models” (LLMs) like OpenAI's General Pre-trained Transformer (GPT) begin to show the ability to generate coherent responses to a variety of user inputs.
- **Fully autonomous car capable of driving safely on any road in any part of the world**
  - Future: does not currently exist.
- **Personal ‘AI milestone’**
  - Reflect on a moment in life that influenced your thinking or understanding about AI.
