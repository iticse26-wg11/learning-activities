# Inductive LLM Explainability Pipeline

**Related ILOs** (at the end of the course, students should be able to …)
- [MM08](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/MM08.md): discuss the limitations of explainability in GenAI, including why outputs can be difficult to interpret, and identify strategies to assess the model's responses (e.g., fact-checking against trusted sources and cross-checking outputs with alternative prompts) despite limited transparency.
- [EPR06](https://github.com/iticse26-wg11/intended-learning-outcomes/blob/main/ilos/EPR06.md): differentiate between reliable, trustworthy, and responsible AI systems.

**Type:** In-class, small groups, digital · **Duration:** 1 hour · **Scale:** Small and large cohorts

## Description

This activity consists of two tasks: research and reflection.

*Task 1 - Research.* The instructor provides students with 10 pre-generated outputs from a GenAI system to fact-check. The students are not expected to generate the initial responses themselves. Instructor-generated outputs should ideally include some that are intentionally false or, in some cases, somewhat difficult to fact-check. Possible themes the instructor can pick from include: information from a trusted source such as a government website, news article, prominent person and location check, soccer player/club.

The students are tasked with working in pairs to discuss whether they were able to determine how GenAI arrived at the output provided, what information about the model’s reasoning, if provided, was visible or hidden, and fact-check the provided outputs. Once initial fact-checking is done, still working in pairs, students attempt to develop and manipulate prompts to search for alternative sources to further verify their results.

*Some of the questions that can be asked as part of the fact-checking process are:*

- Who is/are the author(s)?
- What is/are the evidence(s) provided?
- What are credible sources for this type of information and what do they say?
- Has the information been verified against at least two independent and credible resources?
- What is the publication date?
- Is there any record or evidence supporting or contradicting the claim?
- Has there been any text searches or reverse image searches that verify the claim?

*Task 2 - Reflection* The instructor provides students with definitions of reliable, trustworthy, and responsible GenAI systems. Using the fact-checking example above, they assess the reliable characteristics of the output they received. The session closes with the instructor leading a discussion on the difference between reliable, trustworthy, and responsible GenAI systems. The instructor also provides examples of such system/frameworks/models.
